Panini's Transformations — ϕ: ℝ² → ℝ²

Like the Sanskrit grammarian Panini, who wrote rules that turn one word into another, a linear map ϕ turns each vector (x, y) into a new vector. Here ϕ(x, y) = (2x + 3y, 3x + 4y). The whole behaviour of ϕ is captured by a single 2 × 2 matrix.

ϕ(x, y) = (2x + 3y, 3x + 4y) = A · (x, y)ᵀ with A = [[2, 3], [3, 4]]

Connection to the previous question

The previous question: find x such that g(x) = x³ − x² − 10x + 2 = −22. That's a non-linear inverse — only trial-and-error or Newton's method works.
ϕ is linear, so its inverse is also linear. The matrix A captures ϕ completely, and det(A) ≠ 0 guarantees ϕ⁻¹ exists.
